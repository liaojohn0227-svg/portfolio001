Project 01

Warmth Within the Scars

A children's home for healing and rebuilding
Year
2026
Type
Children's home
Location
Tamsui, New Taipei
Course
Graduation Design
Instructor
Prof. Chen Shih-Tang

According to Taiwan's Ministry of Health and Welfare, more than 4,500 children and young people are placed in care every year.

The need for placement is not falling but rising. At the same time, foster parents are growing older, and their numbers are declining year after year. In some residential care institutions, each caregiver looks after as many as six children, and both staffing and resources remain unstable.

Caring for one child costs as much as NT$70,000 a month, yet government support covers only about NT$30,000, leaving a clear gap.

Children's homes today face several problems at once: too few staff, too little funding, and limited capacity for care.

So what is a children's home? It takes in children and young people who have lost family support, and gives them daily care, education and counselling. Today it is officially a residential care institution for children and youth. It focuses on family-style care and helps children prepare to return to their birth families or to be adopted, rather than serving as an orphanage in the old sense.

This change in role and form makes it even harder to design a children's home that lives up to these expectations.

“The workshop courtyard is the geometric core. It filters the harsh sunset glare into soft light, which deepens, layer by layer, into the quiet shade of the inner court.”

“By dividing the housing units into small geometric parts, the design breaks up the uniform feel of a traditional institution and creates layers of living space that give each child a sense of belonging.”

Facade: Scars and Stitching

The facade uses rough red brick to carry on the historic texture of Tamsui, woven together with modern white concrete. Where the two materials interlock, they stand for the healing of the heart.

Geometric cracks cut into the building's skin let it breathe with the breeze and temper its microclimate. The harsh western sun is cut and filtered by the facade, becoming soft patches of light inside. It is a protective wall, and also a standalone landscape that bears its scars yet lets light through everywhere.
